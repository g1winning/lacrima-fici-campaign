Venue and buyer research: method and how to use the list

Prepared 24 October 2026 as market research on the supply chain between the orchard and the glass. The named people, their emails, their phone numbers sit in a private spreadsheet, lacrima_fici_contacts_classified.csv, held by Luca for Santo and the agency. This page records how the list was built so the executive team can judge it, and what to do with it.

What was asked

Top-level contacts within the supply chain, with a focus on Shepparton, Carlton, the Mornington Peninsula, the Surf Coast; large hospitality venues; highly rated venues which do cocktails; ice creameries as a customer.

How the list was built

The prospecting database holds companies with a public business profile, plus the people who list themselves as working there. It filters by Australian state rather than town, so the search took Victoria across eleven categories: restaurants; bars, taverns, nightclubs; hotels, motels; hospitality; accommodation; food service; food retail; grocers; wineries; distilleries; food wholesale. It then narrowed to businesses whose websites mention Shepparton, Goulburn Valley, Carlton, Lygon Street, Mornington Peninsula, Mornington, Sorrento, Red Hill, Flinders, Surf Coast, Torquay, Lorne, Anglesea, cocktail, gelato, ice cream or cheese.

That returned 2,088 businesses, of which the 300 largest were sampled. Two lists of people were drawn from those 300, limited to people with an email or phone on file: 125 owners, founders, directors, partners, chief officers; 73 head chefs, executive chefs, beverage or bar managers, purchasing or procurement managers, venue managers, general managers. After removing duplicates there are 190 people at 114 businesses. A verified work email exists for 188 of them; a mobile number for six. The export cost 917 credits against a quoted 2,184, because the tool charges only for contacts it finds.

What the list is good for, and what it is not

Each person is tagged in the private file by the kind of business they work for: venue or venue group, retail shelf, caterer, distributor, peer producer, or not a fit. The venue rows are the buyer's-box targets for the hospitality channel. The distributor rows are the multiplier: City Larder, Gippsland Cheese, 5Ways Foodservice, My Local Foodie each supply dozens of venues, two of them with a purchasing or procurement head on the list. The peer producers are not customers, but several, Mingle Seasoning or Honest Eggs among them, have made the journey from farmers' market to supermarket shelf in the last five years; they would take a call from Santo.

The database is strong on groups and companies large enough to have a staff page. It is weak on single-site independents, so Shepparton is almost empty (The Trawool Estate at Seymour is the nearest venue on the list), while the Surf Coast returns resorts or chains rather than the Torquay deli. Those targets come from local knowledge, from the main street, not from a database. The seniority search also reads "director" literally, so it returned HR, data or sales directors at larger companies; they are kept in the file, marked, in case a head of sales at a hospitality group is the right door, but excluded from the public tables.

How to use it

Treat it as a research list, not a mailing list. Under the Spam Act a commercial email to a work address at a business is permitted where the address is published or the message relates to the person's role, but one unsolicited message from Santo about a sample is the limit; anything resembling a campaign needs consent. The right use is the buyer's box: pick twenty venues from the tables, post the box to the named head chef or beverage manager with the handwritten card, then call ten days later. For the distributors, the ask is a conversation with the purchasing head about carrying the line, margin sheet attached.

Cost and repeatability

The whole exercise used 917 credits of the 2,786 on the account. The same search can be rerun for New South Wales or South Australia at a similar cost, refined by company size to favour independents, if the Victorian list proves useful.
