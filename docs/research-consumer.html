Product placement, "pantry" segments and sponsorship: the rules. All from the SBS Commercial, Funding and External Relationships Guidelines (April 2022), fetched at the PDF URL above.

Question What the guidelines say
Can a brand pay to appear in a program? Paid inclusion of a product is "brand integration" and is treated as third party funding (3.4, 3.2). It needs written approval through SBS commissioning, case by case, and SBS keeps editorial control.
Can a brand supply product free for use on set? Yes, with "sufficient editorial justification", disclosure and approval by the editorial manager. The product must not be "used in the content in a way that actively promotes" it (3.4).
Credits Must acknowledge help but "must not promote or endorse the organisation" (3.5).
"As seen on SBS" claims SBS will not approve statements that "amount to advertising such as 'as seen on SBS'" (5.2). Do not plan packaging or social copy around this.
Sponsorship Allowed, including naming rights and category exclusivity, sold by SBS Media (4.5). No SBS Food program sponsorship package is published.
Competitions and giveaways SBS can run its own or joint giveaways with external organisations. The donor's product cannot be endorsed or recommended in the announcement (4.10).
Payment Cash, not contra, for advertising and sponsorship (4.1.1).

Conclusion on pantry segments. I found no SBS Food "pantry" or product segment format. A paid on-screen product feature is possible only as a negotiated brand integration with no active promotion, which is unlikely to suit a low budget. The cheapest plausible route is free product supplied through the program's normal channels, or a giveaway coordinated with SBS. Whether SBS Food runs giveaways in 2027 is UNVERIFIED. The route to ask: SBS help form (https://help.sbs.com.au/hc/en-au/requests/new) and the SBS Media form.

What this means for the pitch

Lead with the Shepparton fig story, not paid media. The Romeo family farm has already had Shepparton News coverage (April 2024), so a local launch story is the cheapest first win, with ABC Shepparton alongside it.

Spend effort where producers are already the subject: The Producers and Dirty Linen (one contact form reaches both), ABC RN Every Bite, Triple R Eat It, and Broadsheet's Dan Cunningham, who explicitly takes food products. Richard Cornish, Dani Valent, Jill Dupleix and Lee Tran Lam are approachable as journalists; send product only after a short email.

Treat SBS as a long shot. No minimum or rate is published, SBS bans "as seen on SBS" claims, and paid product integration needs formal approval. Ask SBS Media for a quote, but plan without it.

Skip the big-name paid route: Adam Liaw, Justine Schofield, Nagi and others above 100K are priced for agencies. If paying, use A$100 to A$500 micro creators and disclose.
