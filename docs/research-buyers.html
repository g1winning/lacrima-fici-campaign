1.2 Coles, Coles Local and the Nurture Fund

  • Coles publishes a five step supplier path: review the retail readiness checklist, engage the buying team, complete the New Supplier Registration form (only if a product is accepted), registration review, then set-up. Source: https://suppliercentral.coles.com.au/s/how-to-become-a-coles-supplier (read 24 Oct 2026). The FAQ on that page says a category manager reviews the application and contacts the supplier if more is needed. This is Coles' own public page; there is no named buyer contact.
  • Coles' preferred route to its buyers is RangeMe. Coles page on RangeMe: https://www.rangeme.com/coles (read 24 Oct 2026). Coles' own onboarding Step 2 article says Coles "has partnered with RangeMe" and that profile products become visible to category buyers: https://suppliercentral.coles.com.au/s/article/Supplier-Onboarding-Step-2-Engage-with-our-buying-team-to-express-your-interest/ (search excerpt, read 24 Oct 2026). A direct approach (trade show, email, phone) is allowed, and a secondary source reports Coles asks that it be paired with a RangeMe profile: https://morice.co/guides/how-to-get-a-product-into-coles-or-woolworths (Morice&Co, Melbourne packaging studio, published 7 Oct 2026).
  • RangeMe cost: a free basic plan exists (the Opener blog summary says three published products); paid plans start at US$99 a year for unlimited products and prioritised placement. Sources: https://www.rangeme.com/suppliers and https://blog.getopener.ai/rangeme-premium-cost-visibility-value (search excerpts, 24 Oct 2026). Free-tier limits are UNVERIFIED on the RangeMe page itself.
  • Minimum requirements (both Coles and Woolworths): ABN, GS1 GTIN and barcode, statutory labelling compliance, council registration for food suppliers, continuity of supply, local bank account, signed trading terms. Source: Morice&Co guide above, which cites the Coles Supplier Central readiness checklist.
  • Coles Local is a small-format neighbourhood banner that "ranges premium products from local suppliers" alongside essentials (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coles_Local, read 24 Oct 2026; 31 Coles Local stores in 2025 per https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coles_Supermarkets). Coles lists 12 Victorian Coles Local stores (Glenferrie, Balwyn North, Surrey Hills, Daylesford, Fitzroy, St Kilda, two in South Melbourne, Brighton, Toorak Village, Camberwell, Glen Iris): https://www.coles.com.au/about/our-businesses/coles-local (read 24 Oct 2026). Coles' own submission to the ACCC states Coles Local products are also supplied at Coles Supermarkets with the same state-based promotional activity (https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/Coles_Final.pdf, excerpt, 24 Oct 2026). Coles' SA store opening release cites "over 700 South Australian favourites from more than 60 local suppliers" (https://www.colesgroup.com.au/news/2025/media-releases/?page=theres-a-new-local-in-town--south-australias-first-coles-local-store-arrives-in-adelaide, 2025). I found no separate public Coles Local ranging program. The pathway appears to be the standard Coles route with a local-supplier story. UNVERIFIED.
  • Coles Nurture Fund: a $50 million fund giving annual grants to small and medium Australian food businesses for innovation, technology, sustainability and unique products. Official: https://www.coles.com.au/nurturefund (referenced from https://suppliercentral.coles.com.au/s/article/https-www-coles-com-au-about-sustainability-better-together-farmers-and-suppliers-coles-nurture-fund, article dated 11 Feb 2026). Eligibility reported as under $25 million revenue and 50 or fewer full-time staff (MP post, https://www.facebook.com/MichaelCrandonMP/posts/coles-nurture-fund-the-coles-nurture-fund-is-now-accepting-applications-from-sma/1295253049091234/, date not shown; confirm on the official page). A Coles post states applicants need not be Coles suppliers (https://www.facebook.com/coles/posts/the-coles-nurture-fund-is-supporting-aussie-farmers-like-clinton-/1460041812819787/). It is a capital grant, not a listing mechanism. Current round dates are UNVERIFIED (a grants consultancy lists Round 14: https://pattens.com/grants/coles-nurture-fund/).

1.3 Woolworths, Metro and local sourcing

  • Woolworths Partner Hub six step path: register, submit product for consideration, complete account and enrich article if accepted, sign Trading Terms, supplier number issued, finalise article. Requirements include ABN, GTIN, statutory labelling, signed Trading Terms, and continuity of supply. Source: https://partnerhub.woolworthsgroup.com.au/s/how-to-become-a-woolworths-supplier (read 24 Oct 2026). Category Managers or Buyers run "rigorous product reviews each year" (same page).
  • Range review timing: the Range Review Calendar lists the dates new product submissions are due to the Category Manager and when visual planograms are due to stores; dates sit behind Partner Hub and are "updated regularly" (article last modified 20 Jul 2026): https://partnerhub.woolworthsgroup.com.au/s/article/Range-Review-Calendar. Secondary source: reviews happen "once or twice every year", with off-cycle submissions "only in exceptional circumstances" (Morice&Co, 7 Oct 2026). I could not see the condiments or syrups sub-category date without a login.
  • Webforms (free browser ordering) takes two to three weeks to schedule; EDI via the B2B gateway takes eight to sixteen weeks (Morice&Co, 7 Oct 2026, secondary).
  • Woolworths Metro: no separate public ranging pathway found. The ACCC reported Woolworths earns higher margins at its smaller-format Metro stores (ABC summary of the ACCC final report, 20 Mar 2025: https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-03-20/woolworths-coles-accc-supermarkets-inquiry/105077616). Woolworths states its values include "source locally" on Partner Hub, but I found no named local-supplier program for packaged goods. UNVERIFIED.

1.4 Metcash, IGA and Ritchies

  • IGA stores are independently owned. Metcash told the ACCC that, except in limited circumstances, retailers have no obligation to buy only from Metcash, that retailers "may make purchases from local suppliers in local markets", that about 74% of IGA purchases go through Metcash, and that in fresh food independents source 50 to 90% from alternatives "with a strong preference for local producers". 1.6 Dan Murphy's, BWS and the cocktail mixer question

  • Dan Murphy's does stock cordials and syrups as a category: Monin Pomegranate Syrup 700 mL at $19.50 and Sticky syrups appear at https://www.danmurphys.com.au/other-drinks/cordials-syrups (search excerpt, read 24 Oct 2026) and https://www.facebook.com/Stickylollies/posts/find-syrups-by-sticky-online-now-at-dan-murphys-we-are-so-excited-to-be-apart-of/1335250268643599/. So a fig syrup has a plausible shelf and editorial home as a cocktail ingredient.
  • Pathway: Endeavour Group and Woolworths share Partner Hub, and the Endeavour "Liquor Submit a Product for Review" article points to an Endeavour Supplier Support Guide (article dated 1 Apr 2026): https://partnerhub.woolworthsgroup.com.au/s/article/Liquor-Submit-a-Product-for-Review. Endeavour Marketplace (drop-ship online ranging) states it is "no longer accepting applications" and redirects to the Endeavour Partner Portal new product submission form: https://endeavourmarketplace.com.au/ (read 24 Oct 2026). Endeavour's earlier news says 14-day payment terms are permanently available to smaller suppliers (https://www.endeavourgroup.com.au/news-and-media/endeavour-group-hosts-first-supplier-forum-for-2024-with-launch-of-category-specific-deep-dives, 2024, excerpt).
  • BWS: no evidence found of BWS stocking food-type syrups. UNVERIFIED.
  • GST point that affects the whole pitch: the ATO treats "syrups for flavouring food" (maple syrup, ice-cream topping) as GST-free and "syrups for use to flavour beverages" as taxable, judged on the product's essential character including marketing and packaging (ATO detailed food list, https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?DocID=GII/GSTIIFL1/NAT/ATO/00001, read 24 Oct 2026). Positioning Lacrima Fici as a cocktail or drinks syrup could make it GST-taxable; positioning it as a food condiment (cheese, breakfast, dessert) keeps it GST-free. Confirm with the accountant before labels and trade copy lock.

2.2 What your $7.50 / $12.00 price implies (my arithmetic)

Scenario Retailer buys at Retailer margin on $12.00 RRP
Direct to deli, GST-free $7.50 37.5% (markup 1.6x)
Direct, GST applies (RRP ex GST $10.91) $7.50 31.3%
Via distributor adding 15% $8.63 28.1%
Via distributor adding 20% $9.00 25.0%
Via distributor adding 30% $9.75 18.8%
Supermarket takes 10% everyday discount $6.75 43.8%
Supermarket takes 20% everyday discount $6.00 50.0%

The $7.50 price sits just under the 40 to 50% deli expectation (UNVERIFIED) and needs a 20% everyday discount to meet the 50% supermarket figure. If a distributor is added without lifting RRP, retailer margin falls to 19 to 28%. To hold 40% retailer margin through a 20% distributor, RRP would need to be $15.00.

What this means for the pitch

The realistic first listings by February 2027 are independent delis, gourmet grocers (Leo's, farm-gate, gift shops), direct-to-store IGAs and Harris Farm. Coles and Woolworths work on category review calendars, need a GTIN and trading terms, and take weeks to transact, so build a RangeMe profile now and aim at their next reviews, not February.

Price is the main objection to prepare for. At $7.50 wholesale and $12.00 RRP a deli earns 37.5%, just under the 40 to 50% commonly expected (not verified for Australia). Any distributor on top squeezes retailer margin to 19 to 28%. Lead trade copy with the margin per case of 12 ($54) and offer a first-order incentive or a distributor-free direct price.

Fit your story to the shelf. As a food condiment (cheese, breakfast, dessert) the syrup is GST-free; as a drinks syrup it is taxable. Dan Murphy's does range syrups, so a cocktail angle is available, but pick one primary positioning.

Send the Metcash EOI after 26 October. Plan C&I Expo (16 to 17 March 2027) as the first trade-floor moment, and enter the Australian Food Awards syrup class in mid-2027.
