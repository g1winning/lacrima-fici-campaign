Consumer campaign copy

Consumer activity starts the week of 15 February 2027, the first week of the Victorian fig harvest, and runs to Easter on 28 March. Everything below is written to that window.

1. Consumer media release

For release Monday 15 February 2027.

The tear of the fig: a Shepparton family bottles Australia's pure fig syrup.

At the Romeo family orchard on Central Avenue, Shepparton, the Black figs ripen in the February heat until they split at the eye and weep a bead of syrup. For three generations the family sold the fruit fresh. The figs that split, or carried a sun spot, were left for the birds.

Lacrima Fici, Latin for tear of the fig, is what became of them. The ripe fruit is crushed, warmed gently until it thickens, and bottled. Nothing is added. The syrup is dark amber, less sweet than it looks. It goes anywhere maple goes: pancakes, porridge, yoghurt, gelato. It is at its best on cheese, where a pour over blue or aged pecorino does what quince paste does with less effort, and in the kitchen as a glaze for pork or a finish for a stir-fry.

Santo Romeo, whose grandfather planted the first trees in 1944, said the family had watched good fruit go to waste for years. "A fig with a sun spot tastes the same as one without. Now the whole crop is sold."

Lacrima Fici is available from February 2027 in a 200 mL bottle at $12.00 from [ranging retailers] and at the farm gate in Shepparton.

Media: [contact]. Images and the family's story at [URL].

4. Influencer and recipe-creator brief

Who. Melbourne and regional Victorian food creators with 10,000 to 80,000 followers who cook on camera and already post seasonal produce. Twenty to thirty accounts, approached from 1 February so the first posts land in the launch week.

What they receive. Two bottles, a kilogram of fresh figs from the harvest (while it lasts), a card with the family's story and three uses, and the offer of a paid recipe post for the strongest fit. No script.

What is asked. One post or reel in fig season showing the syrup used. The brand tagged. The phrase "tear of the fig" is welcome but not required. Any health claim is not permitted: the syrup is one ingredient with no added sugar, and that is all that may be said about it.

Why it works. A creator with 30,000 engaged followers who cooks on camera sells more bottles than a magazine page at a fraction of the cost, leaving the brand a library of recipe content to repost for a year.

7. In-store tasting script

The tasting is yoghurt or ricotta on a spoon with a thread of syrup, and a cube of blue cheese with a drop for anyone who wants the second taste. The demonstrator says three things and stops.

"This is pure fig syrup from Shepparton, one ingredient. It goes anywhere maple goes. Try it on the cheese as well, that is where it surprises people."

If asked about sugar: "It is the fruit's own sugar, nothing added. We cannot make health claims, but the ingredient list is one word."

If asked about price: "Twelve dollars for 200 mL. It is a fruit syrup, so a little goes further than maple, and it is grown two hours up the road."
