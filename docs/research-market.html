1. Fig syrup competitors sold in Australia

Summary

There is no national-supermarket fig syrup that I could find. The Australian fig syrups are small regional producers selling mostly through their own sites, farmers' markets, delis and regional grocers. At $6.00 per 100 mL, Lacrima Fici's recommended retail sits level with The Figgery (about $5.96 per 100 mL at 250 mL) and just under Hillside Family Grove ($6.40). The absence of a Coles, Woolworths or Harris Farm fig syrup is a finding from my searches, not an exhaustive shelf audit, so treat it as "none found".

Two competitor notes worth flagging to the client

  1. Claim consistency. An Adelaide Central Market listing describes The Figgery syrup as "no added sugar" (https://shop.adelaidecentralmarket.com.au/products/figgery-twin-pack-250ml-sticky-fig-10-off, excerpt, 24 Oct 2026), while The Figgery's own Instagram post says it is made from fresh figs and Australian sugar (https://www.instagram.com/p/CBaU-mbn5nK/, excerpt). I cannot reconcile these. FSANZ notes that "no added sugar" products can still be high in naturally occurring sugars (https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/consumer/nutrition/Sugar). Lacrima Fici should confirm its own ingredient list before using any sugar claim.
  2. The maple comparison is already taken. The Figgery's website pitches its syrup as a more intense alternative to maple syrup. "Fig instead of maple" is therefore not an unclaimed idea, although The Figgery's reach is largely South Australian.

Read-across to Lacrima Fici: at $6.00 per 100 mL, a fig syrup is about 1.6 times Queen (the dearest mainstream pure maple at $3.80), 2.5 times the supermarket own-brand pure maple ($2.40) and 2.7 times Steeves ($2.20). Pure maple is cheap in Australia. A National Post piece (republished by Yahoo, 9 April 2026) quotes a Dalhousie University food economist attributing the low Australian shelf price to efficient sea freight and retailer loss-leader pricing, and notes Australian maple is "highly likely to be 100% maple syrup". A fig syrup cannot compete with maple on price and should not try to.

Calculated from the table (my arithmetic): volume up 19.9% in 2025 over 2024 and 31.4% over 2021; value up 27.1% in 2025 and 59.1% over 2021. Implied import unit value rose from about C$9.05 per kg in 2021 to about C$10.96 per kg in 2025 (+21%). As a scale check (my estimate, indicative only), 3,731 tonnes is roughly 11 million 250 mL bottle equivalents, assuming about 1.33 kg per litre, though that includes bulk and foodservice.

A second source, GTAIC (an automated trade-analysis site), reports Australia's imports for March 2025 to February 2026 at US$30.41 million and 3,881.59 tonnes, up 25.4% and 26.2% on the prior twelve months (https://gtaic.ai/market-reports/maple-sugar-and-maple-syrup-forecast-top-20-importing-countries-world-in-2026, excerpt, 24 Oct 2026). Direction agrees with AAFC. Treat it as secondary.

Supply-side risk. CBC and the Toronto Star reported in October 2026 that Quebec's strategic maple reserve is nearly depleted, with the last stock expected to sell out by the end of February 2027, driven by US demand and tariff stockpiling. Source: https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/montreal/quebec-maple-syrup-reserves-nearly-depleted-9.7386889 and https://www.thestar.com/business/quebec-s-strategic-maple-syrup-reserve-has-run-dry-amid-soaring-u-s-demand-and-trump-tariff-fears/article_c0b1826f-e120-42b8-8c8c-1f8afe93c32f.html (both excerpts; exact publication dates not confirmed, search showed "4 days ago" for CBC). If maple prices rise, the price gap to fig narrows. This is an inference, not a forecast.

6. Glycaemic index of figs and fig syrup

  • Dried figs: the University of Sydney's international GI table lists dried, tenderised figs (Dessert Maid brand, Sydney) at GI 61 plus or minus 6 (glucose = 100), tested in 10 healthy subjects. That is in the medium GI band (56 to 69). Source: International table of glycemic index and glycemic load values 2002, American Journal of Clinical Nutrition, via https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165(23)05840-9/fulltext and the table PDF https://www.yogurtinnutrition.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/gi-table.pdf (excerpt, 24 Oct 2026). The current Sydney database is at https://glycemicindex.com/gi-search/ and the 2021 tables are at https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34258626/; I did not retrieve the 2021 figure for figs.
  • Lower-quality sources that disagree: a glycemic-index.net chart lists dried fig at 50 and a consumer site gives 51 to 65. A Californian fig cooperative repeats GI 61 (https://valleyfig.com/blog/figs-and-blood-sugar-glycemic-index-of-figs/). Do not use these.
  • Fig syrup or petimezi: I found no tested GI value. UNVERIFIED. Mordor's fresh-figs report loosely says fig paste and syrup have a "mid-range" GI, which is an untested generalisation.

Caveat for the client: no GI claim can be made for Lacrima Fici without testing the actual product. A value for dried whole figs does not transfer to a reduced syrup with different composition and any added sugar. Australia and New Zealand regulate GI claims as nutrition content claims and also recognise the GI Foundation's certified Low GI symbol as an endorsement (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8466312/, peer-reviewed review). The University of Sydney's GI Research Service (SUGiRS) has offered commercial GI testing since 1995 (https://glycemicindex.com/glycemic-index-testing-research/). Testing cost and turnaround: UNVERIFIED. Confirm any claim wording with a food regulatory adviser before it appears in copy.

What this means for the pitch

Position Lacrima Fici as a premium Australian pantry and gifting product, not a maple substitute. Pure maple sits at $2.20 to $3.80 per 100 mL; at $6.00 per 100 mL the fig syrup will always look dear on a supermarket shelf, so sell provenance, scarcity and use occasions (cheese, pork, ice cream, cocktails) rather than value.

The competitive gap is geography and scale: the Australian fig syrups found are small regional makers, mostly in South Australia and NSW, and I found no Victorian-grown fig syrup or national supermarket listing. "Black figs from Shepparton" is a distinct claim, provided the family can evidence it.

The retail price matches The Figgery per 100 mL, so there is no price headroom to premiumise without a clear reason. Use the Feb to April harvest as a seasonal hook, and Maggie Beer's stocking of The Figgery as proof that a hamper and gift channel exists.

Avoid sugar, "natural sweetener" health and GI claims until ingredients and testing are confirmed. Mention date syrup only as a category signal, not a growth forecast.
